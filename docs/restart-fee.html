1 Context

The rudraspirit.com project was completed, delivered, and paid in full (₹40,000). Work then paused for roughly 6 months with no active development or feedback.

Because the original scope was fully delivered and paid, there is no outstanding balance and no kill fee involved. Resuming, however, is not a free continuation — a dormant project must be brought back to a working, current, and deployable state before new work can begin. That reactivation effort is what the restart fee covers.

Why an extra charge? The original ₹40,000 paid for building and delivering the site — not for holding it ready indefinitely. In the 6-month gap, resources moved to other clients and the site fell behind on updates and security. Getting it back to a safe, current, deployable state — and reserving time on the schedule again — is fresh work that wasn't part of the completed project. The restart fee pays for that, and nothing more.

This is standard practice: stalled projects are not resumed for free, because resources have been reallocated and pivoting back costs time.

Reactivation (restart) fee — one-time, flat ₹6,000 payable in advance, before work resumes

Basis: ~15% of the original ₹40,000 project value — within the standard 10–20% range for reactivating paused work. This is a flat reactivation charge, not a new-project quote. Reasonable range for this project: ₹4,000 – ₹8,000.

3 What the fee includes

  • Full local environment rebuild
  • Live deployment pipeline setup
  • Core / plugin / theme updates to current, stable versions
  • Security patching and a basic compatibility pass
  • Project placed back on the active schedule

Not included

  • Any new features, redesigns, or scope changes — quoted separately
  • Ongoing maintenance / retainer — optional, see section 5

4 Terms to resume

  1. Restart fee of ₹6,000 payable in advance, before development resumes.
  2. Any new work beyond the original delivered scope is quoted and approved separately.
  3. Response deadline: 5 days from the date above. Without confirmation in 5 days, the project stays closed, no resources are reserved, and a fresh reactivation quote at then-current rates will be required later.

5 Going forward

To prevent uncompensated stalls in future, the following applies to resumed and new work:

Dormancy clause. If the project receives no client feedback or approval for 15 consecutive days, it is automatically marked paused. Resuming requires a restart fee of 10–20% of project value (or a flat reactivation fee), payable before work continues.

After 30 days of inactivity the project may be closed and final-invoiced for work completed to date.

Optional: a monthly maintenance retainer (updates, backups, security, uptime) keeps the site current and avoids future reactivation fees entirely — quote available on request.
